## **Thy Kingdom Come**

### **Radical Teachings From the Sermon on the Mount**

#### By Joe Caruso Stories

When the crowd heard Jesus say the words “Thy kingdom come,” they must have anticipated a political revolution against their oppressors. But Jesus had something else in mind…

An upside-down kingdom governed by sacrificial love.

As Jesus went through Galilee healing the sick, large groups followed him. Seeing the crowds, Jesus climbed a mountainside with his disciples and began to teach. The message is what we know as the Sermon on the Mount.

In the middle of this message is the Lord’s Prayer, where we find the words “Thy kingdom come.” Many of us have heard or even recited this prayer so many times that we take its significance for granted.

But to the crowd gathered at the bottom of the mountainside, the phrase “Thy kingdom come” was anything but insignificant.

To understand the weight of these words, we have to go back to the very beginning of the Bible.

### Heavenly & Earthly Kingdoms

In the beginning, God established a kingdom where his heavenly presence would reside on earth, and humanity would rule according to God’s own wisdom, peace and love.

This was the Garden of Eden, the perfect picture of Jesus’ words in Matthew 6:10.

[![](/images/blog/2026-11-Newsletter-1.png)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=matthew%206%3A10&version=KJV)

But humanity wanted to rule their own kingdom. They quickly rebelled and began building earthly kingdoms of their own. Kingdoms built on sin, power and greed, far from the heavenly kingdom God intended.

So God called a man named Abraham. He made a covenant with Abraham that his descendants would represent God’s kingdom throughout the world. God’s heavenly kingdom would again be established on earth as it is in heaven.

Over the next two thousand years, God’s people were still determined to do things their own way. They cycled through centuries of rebellion, exile, repentance and restoration, only to one day find themselves sitting on a mountainside, listening to Jesus, under the oppressive earthly kingdom of the Roman Empire.

So when the crowd heard Jesus say “Thy kingdom come,” it’s understandable that their hearts swelled with national pride, with visions of regaining their own kingdom as God’s chosen people.

### An Upside-Down Kingdom

This time, however, Jesus was describing an entirely different kind of kingdom.

Jesus’ Sermon on the Mount wasn’t simply a set of rules to follow. Jesus was making the case that there was another way to live that was contrary to any human thinking.

In making his case, Jesus used provocative language to describe this upside-down heavenly kingdom. With statements like “blessed are the poor in spirit,” “if your eye causes you to stumble, gouge it out,” and “love your enemies,” Jesus was planting seeds in the crowd’s hearts to help them contemplate what God’s kingdom looks like.

The following chart highlights some of the radical principles Jesus used when teaching about God’s kingdom here on earth.

![](/images/blog/26-11-1.png)

Jesus was flipping the script on the kind of principles a kingdom is built on. Instead of a kingdom built on power, dominance and pride, he was calling his followers to participate in a kingdom built on self-sacrifice, mercy and love.

### Jesus Demonstrates His Kingdom

Jesus didn’t just describe “Thy kingdom come”; he demonstrated it. Wherever he went, Jesus healed the sick, fed the hungry, opened blind eyes and even raised the dead. He was demonstrating that his kingdom was built on healing and love, not human strength and power.

The ultimate demonstration was the cross. Healing of the soul was Christ’s greatest victory. When Jesus died for our sins and rose again, he defeated sin and death so we could be part of his kingdom and help bring healing to the world.

[![](/images/blog/2026-11-Newsletter-2.png)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%205%3A8&version=NIV)

### Today’s Kingdom Opportunity

Today we have an opportunity to be part of God’s healing and restoration mission. Our world is broken by sin and in desperate need of salvation. We’ve been called to be a light in the darkness, not only to pray, “Thy kingdom come,” but to actively live out the upside-down message Jesus gave in the Sermon on the Mount.

[![](/images/blog/2026-11-Newsletter-3.png)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%205%3A14-16&version=NIV)

By living out Jesus’ teachings, we’re building our lives on him, the rock on which God’s kingdom stands. Every time we choose his upside-down way, we put **_“Thy kingdom come”_** into practice.
